# Known-load control check

A simulation that gives a plausible-looking S11 can still be testing something other than what you built. One generated snapshot grounded its own feed by a 9 micrometre overlap; every run for hours "worked" and said nothing. Run these first, cheapest first. All GPU runs go through the shared lock (CLAUDE.md); the geometry check needs no GPU.

0. **Read the run notes first.** The voxelizer now reports copper of different nets that touch on the grid, a port whose two terminals are joined by copper (or that floats), metal bodies that touch in the grid, and dielectric bodies under two cells across. Any of them on a fresh snapshot is a defect until explained. A via note says whether the via sits on the other net's copper (a real short) or the board keeps them apart and only the grid joins them (a gap under a cell: refine there; do not edit a clean board for it).
1. **Geometry: nets that must be separate are separate.** `python3 .claude/skills/fdtd-control-check/net_separation.py SNAPSHOT.json F.Cu X0 X1 Y0 Y1 0.05 NET_A NET_B` (window in mm around the feed, pads and pour steps; exits 1 and prints where they touch). Check each layer that carries the feed. Look at corners where a wide track meets a clearance step, and at shapes drawn to share an edge: a shared edge or a few-micrometre overlap connects on the grid.
2. **Known loads on the same feed, no device.** Make the snapshot with the part absent, once left open and once shorted by a copper tie from the feed to the pour at the pad, with the port 4 to 6 mm from the pad (a short line keeps phase bias small). Run both, coarse cell is fine (about 80 s). `python3 .claude/skills/fdtd-control-check/phase_fit.py RUN_A RUN_B`: the phase of S11 extrapolated to zero frequency must read about 0 degrees for the open and about 180 for the short (within roughly 45), and the slope must scale with the distance to the termination (slope magnitude = 720 x length x sqrt(eps_eff) / c, in degrees per GHz; about 4.6 per mm was measured on one FR-4 coplanar-with-ground reference stackup). If open and short read the same, the port is not seeing the load: stop and find the break (geometry check, port ends on connected copper, vias, grid snapping) before any device run.
   **Build the controls so they can be read.** (a) End the line at the port: cut the copper beyond it, or the far side of a mid-line port (a long open line) dominates and open and short read alike. (b) The short tie must reach the real pour: the dry run of the short must say "joined by copper" and the open must not; a tie that only touches the feed line is not a short (three runs on one board were never shorts). (c) A coplanar gap (feed line to pour) narrower than one cell joins the nets on the grid, whatever `net_separation.py` says at 0.05 mm: it can only see a gap larger than its raster. If an open control says "joined by copper" or reads short, suspect it first; bisect with dry runs (drop the vias, the pour, one polygon at a time) and put `--refine X0 Y0 X1 Y1 CELL` around the feed with cells of at most a third of the gap. The presets do not resolve it; the smallest cell exists only at the port. The solver's touch note now also fires for nets meeting at one grid node (it once fired only for a shared edge).
3. **Does the quantity you vary matter?** Run the device at two very different values (for example permittivity 4 and 40). Identical S11 means the device is not in the circuit or a feature is unresolved (a thin gap, a thin plate: see below); do not report it as "no dependence".
4. **Resolution of thin features.** The grid snaps to copper corners and to modelled body corners (`snapToGeometry`); a body gap narrower than a cell is otherwise sampled by chance (two mirror-image parts got different cell counts). Check the run notes for each part's cell count, compare mirrored parts, and use `--refine` boxes with cells no larger than the thinnest feature. A null result needs a halved-cell rerun before it is called a null.
   **Air next to the board and gaps.** The air above and below the board is graded from the board's cell size (setting `mesh_growth_ratio`, default 1.3, Mesh group, Advanced); before that default a hard jump to the coarse air cell read a planar line's impedance about 11 percent low (a 50 ohm microstrip came out at 44 ohm, now 50.8). If a run predates it, or the ratio was set to 1, expect that error. A gap between two nets near a port needs about 10 cells across it (3 cells: 15 percent low, 5 cells: 8 percent low): the run notes now say "the gap between nets ... is N cells wide ... near a port" for gaps of 4 cells or fewer; refine there (`--refine`, or Fine cell near ports). Use `python3 scripts/antenna/validate_lines.py` as the worked example of checking a mesh against a closed form.
5. **Convergence before conclusions.** Screening shortcuts (coarse mesh, small boundary distance, 1 % decay stop, cropped board) are for finding bugs and trends only. Rerun the best case at default settings and halve the cell before quoting a number, and say which settings produced it.

Record the controls and their readings in `docs/PLAN.md` next to the result they validate, and keep the control snapshots in `out/` (never /tmp). A control that fails is a finding: write down the cause, not just the fix.
