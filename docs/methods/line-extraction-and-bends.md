# Line extraction from S11, the FDTD solver's own effective permittivity, and right-angle bends

*In-house methods note, 2026-11-23. **Provenance:** the works behind these questions are paywalled and their full text is unread (`docs/methods/paper-claims.md`): Kirschning, Jansen and Koster 1981 (open-end equivalent length; abstract states a maximum relative error under 2.5 percent), Douville and James 1978 (an experimental study of right-angle bends, mitring and rounding, with empirical expressions for the optimum mitre; no numbers in the abstract), Hammerstad and Jensen 1980 (microstrip closed forms with dispersion). Their formulas are not used or compared here. Both methods below are textbook network analysis written here; every number is a measurement of this project's FDTD solver on this project's stackup, not a reproduction of a paper's table.*

## 1. Method A: two-length open-line extraction (`scripts/antenna/line_extract.py`)
A lossless uniform line of length L, impedance Z0, phase constant beta(f) = 2 pi f sqrt(eeff) / c, open at the far end, seen through a port with series inductance Lp: Zin = -j Z0 cot(beta (L + d)) + j w Lp, S11 = (Zin - 50)/(Zin + 50). d is a **common offset**: the open end's fringing, any strip behind the port, and the port's parasitics are all common to two lines of different length on the same board, port and mesh, so the length difference alone fixes beta (the phase of the two curves differs by 2 beta (L2 - L1)). Z0, eeff, d and Lp are fitted to the complex S11 of both lines at once. Tests: exact synthetic data are recovered; with 1 percent noise eeff to 0.05 percent and d to about +-0.3 mm (d is the weakly determined one).

**Robustness on real runs** (2.0 mm line, three straight lengths, 0.1 mm lateral cells): the three pairings give eeff 3.4031 (20/30 mm), 3.4008 (20/24) and 3.4181 (24/30), Z0 63.49, 63.32, 63.51 ohm, d 1.94, 1.90, 1.85 mm: the extraction repeats to about 0.5 percent. The straight model fitted on 20/30 mm predicts an independent 24 mm line with a complex rms error of 0.008. *(An earlier set of results from this study was discarded: three scripts ran at once and shared one snapshot file, so a run waiting for the GPU could read another run's geometry; the scripts now write one file per run and the suspect runs were redone one after another.)*

## 2. The FDTD solver's effective permittivity against quasi-static theory
Quasi-static reference: the 2D solve with distant walls and the closed form agree (`microstrip-closed-forms.md`): eeff 3.082, 3.219, 3.331 for w = 1.0, 2.0, 3.06 mm on 1.6 mm of er 4.4 (closed form).

| line | lateral cell | eeff from the FDTD | Z0 from the FDTD | quasi-static eeff, Z0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 mm | 0.1 mm | 3.344 | 87.1 ohm | 3.082, 87.5 |
| 2.0 mm | 0.1 mm | 3.403 | 63.5 | 3.219, 63.5 |
| 3.06 mm | 0.25 mm | 3.678 | 51.1 | 3.331, 50.0 |
| 2.0 mm | 0.15 mm | 3.467 | 63.8 | 3.219, 63.5 |
| 2.0 mm | 0.2 mm | 3.561 | 64.6 | 3.219, 63.5 |
| 2.0 mm | 0.1 mm, 16 cells through the substrate | 3.401 | 64.2 | 3.219, 63.5 |

* **Vertical resolution is not the cause:** 16 cells through the board instead of 8 changes eeff from 3.403 to 3.401.
* **CORRECTION (2026-11-24): the eeff excess below is mostly the extraction on a wide ground plane, not the solver.** An air-filled line, which must read eeff 1, reads 1.040 on this section's 60 by 30 mm board at every grid variant tried (lateral growth 1.3, 1.1, 1.05; fine air cells; a narrow or board-wide refine box) and 1.0000 plus or minus 0.002 on a 44 by 14 mm board, with a better fit (residual 0.0024 against 0.0065). On the 44 by 14 mm board the er 4.4 line reads 3.378, 3.2975, 3.2678 at 0.2, 0.1, 0.05 mm (limit about 3.25, within 0.6 percent of quasi-static plus dispersion) and Z0 63.5 ohm. So: use a ground plane only a few substrate heights wider than the line for a line study; the lateral-cell numbers below on the wide board, their extrapolated 3.36, the 'tension' with the antenna and the end extensions d and bend values fitted from them (sections 3 to 5) carry that bias and should be read as upper bounds on the solver's error until redone on the narrow board.
* **Lateral cell is:** eeff falls 3.561, 3.467, 3.403 as the cell goes 0.2, 0.15, 0.1 mm, an excess over quasi-static of 10.6, 7.7 and 5.7 percent. The increments for equal steps of 0.05 mm are 0.095 and 0.063, a ratio of 1.50; a pure first-order error would give 1.0 and a second-order one 1.4, so the observed order is about 2.2 and the extrapolated limit is **about 3.36, roughly 4 percent above the quasi-static value**. That residual is larger than any dispersion at 2 GHz (below) and is not explained: it is an **open question about the solver**, not a rounding of the discretisation. Next experiment (not run; two runs of about 26 M cells, an hour each): the same 2.0 mm pair at 0.05 mm cells, which would show whether the limit is 3.36 or lower.
* **A tension to resolve:** the inverted-F antenna moved only 0.4 percent in frequency from 0.2 mm to 0.1 mm cells, while this line's eeff moved 4.6 percent (about 2.3 percent in phase velocity) over the same step. An antenna is not a line, but the difference is not understood.
* **What it means for antennas:** a line-dominated resonance is lowered by about half the permittivity error: the coarse mesh put one inverted-F resonance 4.5 percent low, while 0.2 mm cells were converged to 0.4 percent on that antenna (see the tension above). The practical rule is lateral cells of at most a twentieth of the strip width along a line whose electrical length matters, **which on this study's own data still reads 5.7 percent high in eeff** (0.1 mm on a 2.0 mm line), and a half-cell rerun before quoting a frequency.
* **Dispersion** (Hammerstad and Jensen's abstract claims a model of it) could not be confirmed: a fitted slope of eeff against frequency came out negative (-0.03 to -0.04 per GHz), the wrong sign for a physical line, so the fit's linear term is absorbing something else (the grid, the radiation of the open end); the constant-eeff fit is what is reported. A rough estimate from a dispersion formula recalled from memory (not verified, not from a source read here) puts the physical rise of eeff at 2 GHz on this stackup well under 1 percent, below this extraction's resolution; so dispersion is neither confirmed nor excluded by these runs.

## 3. The common end offset d and the open-end formulas (Kirschning, Jansen and Koster 1981; Hammerstad 1981)
d = 1.76 mm (1.0 mm line), 1.94 mm (2.0 mm), 2.19 mm (3.06 mm). It is the open end **plus** the 0.5 mm of strip behind the port **plus** the port, and the method cannot separate the open end from the port by length alone.

**Formulas used (second-hand, stated plainly):** the Qucs technical documentation's "Microstrip open" page (eqs. 11.185 to 11.191, read in full; its equation images were read where the text was truncated) gives the Kirschning-Jansen-Koster extension (accuracy it states: under 2.5 percent for 0.01 <= W/h <= 100, 1 <= er <= 50) and Hammerstad's (under 1.7 percent for W/h < 20). The papers themselves are unread; `scripts/antenna/openend.py` implements the page's formulas. For this stackup the two models agree with each other within 4 percent (0.49 and 0.49 mm at 1.0 mm width, 0.58 and 0.57 at 2.0, 0.64 and 0.62 at 3.06).

**Measurement (2.0 mm line, 0.1 mm cells):** with 1.0 mm of strip behind the port instead of 0.5 mm, d rose from 1.94 to 2.40 mm (+0.46 for +0.5 mm; eeff 3.402 either way): the strip behind the port is additive to within about 0.04 mm. A third case with no strip behind the port did not fit (rms 0.47; the port then sits on the strip's edge) and is **not** a result. Subtracting the strip behind the port leaves 1.44 mm (0.5 mm case) and 1.40 mm (1.0 mm case) for the open end plus the port, against 0.58 mm predicted for the open end: an unexplained excess of about 0.8 mm, and a similar 0.7 to 1.0 mm excess at the other two widths (1.26 and 1.69 mm remaining against 0.49 and 0.64).

**What this does and does not say:** the open end alone is still not measured, so the 2.5 percent claim is **not tested**. The excess is a property of the port plus open end in this FDTD model, with the lateral-cell permittivity excess of section 2 a possible contributor; it is not evidence against the formula. Separating them needs an end that differs while the port stays the same (a wide pad or a different termination) or a finer mesh: not done.

## 4. Method B: a right-angle bend as a shunt capacitance and a length shift (`scripts/antenna/bend_extract.py`)
One corner after leg1 = 10 mm (port to the crossing of the center lines), then leg2 = 14 mm to the open end, 2.0 mm strip, 1.6 mm of er 4.4, 0.1 mm cells. The straight-line parameters come from method A on the same board and mesh; only the corner is fitted to the S11 of the bent line (complex, 0.8 to 2.2 GHz). A first model, a T of series inductance and shunt capacitance at the crossing, fitted with **negative** L and C for every mitre (L = -0.74 nH): the corner is a shorter path than the center lines suggest, which a lumped T can only say with negative values. The model that carries a physical meaning is a **shunt capacitance Cb plus a signed length shift of the line after the corner** (a pure delay, which does not reflect): only Cb reflects. The straight model predicts a straight 24 mm line (the bend's total length) to 0.008 rms, and every bend fit has rms 0.0036.

Mitre definition used in the table: the cut along each outer strip edge is a = k w. The usual mitre fraction (cut length along the corner square's diagonal over the diagonal w sqrt(2)) is m = k / 2.

| k = a/w | m = k/2 | Cb (pF) | length shift (mm) | the capacitance alone reflects at 2.44 GHz |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (square corner) | 0 | 0.1730 | -1.994 | -21.5 dB |
| 0.3 | 0.15 | 0.1625 | -1.993 | -22.1 |
| 0.5 | 0.25 | 0.1487 | -1.988 | -22.8 |
| 0.7 | 0.35 | 0.1285 | -1.968 | -24.1 |
| 0.85 | 0.425 | 0.1123 | -1.973 | -25.3 |
| 0.95 | 0.475 | 0.0977 | -1.945 | -26.5 |
| 1.15 | 0.575 | 0.0591 | -1.826 | -30.8 |
| 1.35 | 0.675 | 0.0144 | -1.691 | -43.1 |
| 1.6 | 0.80 | -0.0588 | -1.417 | -30.9 |

(statistical spread from the fit +-0.0014 pF and +-0.006 mm; the mesh is a further, unquantified, error.)

**Reading.** (1) A square corner on this line is a shunt capacitance of 0.17 pF (about 1.8 mm of extra line capacitance, 0.097 pF/mm) and an **advance**: the electrical path is 2.0 mm shorter than the center lines (the shift is negative). That is the usual reference-plane convention seen from the other side: moving the planes from the crossing of the center lines to the strip's edges, w/2 back on each leg, removes w = 2.0 mm of line, which is the shift of the square corner; so Cb is the excess capacitance between those planes. The shift drifts to -1.4 mm as the mitre grows because the residual series inductance of the mitred corner is absorbed by it. (2) Mitring removes the capacitance monotonically; it crosses zero at k = 1.40 (m = 0.70, interpolated between k = 1.35 and 1.6; statistical +-0.02 in k) and goes negative (a net inductive corner) beyond, so the capacitance-compensated mitre for this line is about 70 percent, where the corner reflects least (-43 dB at the sampled k = 1.35; a square corner reflects -21.5 dB at 2.44 GHz, 0.084). (3) The length shift shrinks from -2.0 to -1.4 mm as the mitre grows: the cut makes the path longer again. This is the experiment that the Douville-James abstract describes (equivalent-circuit parameters of right-angle corners, the effect of mitring, an optimum mitre), measured on one width and one substrate; their expressions for the optimum are unread and are **not compared**, so agreement or disagreement with them is not claimed. **Limits:** one line width and permittivity; a lumped model fitted over a band; the FDTD mesh and the 5 to 10 percent excess in the line's eeff (section 2), which the straight reference absorbs because both come from the same solver; no rounded corner.

## 5. Use
Feed-line corners on a 2.4 GHz board: a square corner on a 63 ohm line is a 0.17 pF shunt capacitance (about -21.5 dB if nothing else) and takes 2 mm off the electrical length measured along the center lines, a 70 percent mitre is nearly invisible; the feed report names a corner and says how much it matters (`checkFeed`), `cascade.py` sizes steps and stubs, and this note says what a mitre buys. Rerun with `validate_lines.py --case bend --miter K` and `bend_extract.py`.
